# Sugar Futures Research Sources

This reference defines preferred sources for research related to ICE White Sugar No. 5 futures.

Use the highest-quality and most direct source available. Do not treat all sources as equally reliable.

## Source Priority

Prefer sources in approximately this order:

1. Exchanges, regulators, governments, and official statistics
2. Sugar industry associations and crop agencies
3. Established commodity and financial news organizations
4. Specialist agricultural and sugar publications
5. Company reports and trading-house commentary
6. Analyst commentary
7. Social media, forums, Reddit, and other community sources

Social-media information may provide useful leads or market sentiment, but important factual claims should be corroborated whenever possible.

## ICE White Sugar No. 5

### ICE

Primary source for:

- White Sugar No. 5 contract specifications
- Contract months
- Expiry information
- Exchange notices
- Delivery information
- Futures market information

Domain: `ice.com`

When discussing a futures price, identify the relevant contract month whenever possible.

Do not confuse ICE White Sugar No. 5 with ICE Raw Sugar No. 11.

## Brazil

### UNICA

Domain: `unica.com.br`

Prioritize for:

- Center-South cane crush
- Sugar production
- Ethanol production
- Sugar/ethanol production mix
- ATR
- Crushing progress

Pay attention to whether data refers to a two-week period, cumulative crop year, or comparison with the previous season.

### CONAB

Domain: `conab.gov.br`

Prioritize for:

- Sugarcane crop estimates
- Production forecasts
- Yield estimates
- Area estimates
- National and regional crop outlook

Also monitor official Brazilian trade statistics for sugar exports, destinations, and volumes.

## India

Prioritize official Indian government announcements for:

- Sugar export policy
- Export quotas
- Ethanol policy
- Cane policy
- Government production estimates

Government domain: `gov.in`

Also use the Indian Sugar & Bio-energy Manufacturers Association (ISMA) for production estimates, crushing progress, industry commentary, and ethanol diversion estimates.

Where government and industry estimates differ, report both.

## Thailand

Prioritize Thai government and sugar-industry sources for:

- Cane crushing
- Sugar production
- Crop forecasts
- Export availability
- Government policy

Where reliable primary data is unavailable, corroborate specialist reporting with multiple sources.

## European Union

Use European Commission information for:

- EU sugar production
- Beet acreage
- Agricultural policy
- Imports and exports
- Market statistics

Domain: `ec.europa.eu`

Monitor major producing countries including France and Germany when relevant.

## United Kingdom

Prioritize official UK agricultural information and major industry sources for:

- Sugar beet acreage
- Crop conditions
- Beet yields
- UK sugar production
- Import requirements

Clearly distinguish UK information from EU data.

## USDA

Domain: `usda.gov`

Use for:

- Global sugar production
- Consumption
- Imports
- Exports
- Stocks
- Country balance sheets
- World sugar outlook

Check publication dates because USDA estimates may lag faster-moving crop developments.

## Positioning

### CFTC

Domain: `cftc.gov`

Use Commitment of Traders data when relevant.

Remember that ICE White Sugar No. 5 trades in London and should not be confused with US-listed Raw Sugar No. 11.

Raw Sugar No. 11 positioning can nevertheless provide useful context about speculative positioning in the broader sugar complex.

## Energy

Monitor:

- Brent crude oil
- WTI crude oil
- Gasoline
- Brazilian ethanol
- Ethanol parity

Energy markets can affect Brazilian mills' economic incentives between sugar and ethanol.

Do not automatically assume higher crude oil means higher sugar prices. Explain the actual transmission mechanism.

## Currency

Pay particular attention to BRL/USD.

A weaker Brazilian real can increase the local-currency value of dollar-denominated sugar exports, but the actual market effect depends on hedging, production economics, and other conditions.

Also monitor INR, THB, EUR, GBP, and the US dollar when materially relevant.

## Weather

Monitor weather affecting:

- Brazil Center-South cane
- India cane regions and monsoon
- Thailand cane regions
- European sugar beet
- UK sugar beet

Separate:

- Observed weather
- Short-term forecasts
- Seasonal forecasts
- Estimated crop impacts

Do not treat a weather forecast as a confirmed crop loss.

## Trade and Physical Market

Research when available:

- Sugar exports
- Import demand
- Physical premiums
- White premium
- Refinery demand
- Vessel lineups
- Port congestion
- Freight
- Tenders
- Destination demand

Pay particular attention to major importing regions such as China, Indonesia, the Middle East, and North Africa.

## Social and Community Sources

Reddit, X, YouTube, forums, and other community sources can be useful for:

- Emerging discussion
- Potential new developments
- Trader sentiment
- Finding subjects requiring further investigation

They should not normally be the sole evidence for production figures, government policy, export decisions, crop damage, futures prices, or official statistics.

Verify material claims against higher-quality sources whenever possible.

## Research Rules

For every material claim, try to capture:

1. Publication date
2. Event or data date
3. Source
4. Country or region
5. Relevant crop season
6. Whether the information is confirmed, estimated, forecast, commentary, or rumor

Be particularly careful with old information resurfacing on social media.

A recently published article may discuss older data. Distinguish the publication date from the date of the underlying information.

When sources disagree, report the disagreement rather than silently choosing one estimate.

The objective is not to collect the largest number of articles. The objective is to identify the most decision-relevant new information affecting ICE White Sugar No. 5 futures.
